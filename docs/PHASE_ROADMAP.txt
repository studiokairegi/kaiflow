# KairFlow Intelligence — Revised Implementation Roadmap

**Studio Kairegi | 6 November 2026**

**ONE PHASE PER CLAUDE SESSION • SMALL SCOPE • TESTED BEFORE HANDOFF**

## Implementation rules

The phases below must be completed in the exact numbered order. Do not reorder them based on technical dependencies or combine phases.

Each phase must:

- Fit into one manageable Claude session.
- Have one clearly defined objective.
- Inspect only the files and dependencies relevant to that objective.
- Make the smallest necessary changes.
- Add or update targeted tests.
- Run relevant regression tests.
- Inspect the final diff.
- Produce a short handoff report and file that changed only.

Do not attempt to complete several phases in one session.

If a phase becomes too large, stop, explain why, and propose splitting that phase into smaller sub-phases. Do not consume the remaining session budget attempting to finish an oversized task.

The next phase must not begin until the previous phase has been reviewed and accepted.

**Global restrictions:**

- Never modify the `indie_game_developer` qualification path while working on studio scoring.
- Preserve the existing Supabase, worker, CRM, authorization and email architecture.
- Do not introduce paid AI services.
- Do not deploy, push, activate the worker, enable autonomy or send real emails.
- Preserve human approval, kill switch, suppression, unsubscribe and daily limits.
- Do not remove existing functionality without demonstrating why it is necessary.

## PHASE 1 — Fix studio qualification criteria and scoring

**Objective:** Make KairFlow Intelligence correctly recognize suitable animation studios, including small independent studios that are not hiring or advertising outsourcing opportunities.

### Required scoring model

| Criterion                                 | Maximum points |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| Pipeline fit                              |             65 |
| 2D animation relevance                    |             15 |
| External collaboration suitability        |             15 |
| English/Japanese communication preference |              5 |
| **Total**                                 |        **100** |

### Rules

- Pipeline fit must be based on actual production capabilities and evidence.
- Relevant capabilities include layout, key animation, in-betweens, douga, coloring, finishing, character animation and 2D anime production.
- Recruitment and outsourcing announcements are supplementary signals only.
- A studio must not need to be hiring to qualify.
- No minimum employee count, production volume or project count.
- Small and independent studios must remain eligible.
- Global targeting.
- English and Japanese are preferences, not mandatory requirements.
- Uncertainty in pipeline fit, 2D relevance or external collaboration suitability forces Needs Review, even when the numerical score is 70 or higher.

Thresholds:

- 70–100: Qualified on suitability.
- 50–69: Needs Review.
- Below 50: Rejected.

### Session scope

1. Inspect the existing studio scoring functions and signals.
2. Identify which existing signals can support the new criteria.
3. Propose a defensible breakdown of the 65 pipeline-fit points using actual available evidence.
4. Do not invent unsupported scoring categories.
5. Implement the approved studio-only scoring model.
6. Add uncertainty handling and evidence traceability.
7. Preserve old scores and identify the scoring-model version.
8. Test the new scoring against representative studios.

### Acceptance criteria

- The weights total exactly 100.
- A suitable studio without recruitment or outsourcing announcements can qualify.
- Recruitment alone cannot qualify a studio.
- Uncertain evidence produces Needs Review.
- Every awarded point is explainable.
- Existing indie qualification tests pass unchanged.
- Previously scored studio prospects are not silently overwritten or discarded.

**Deliverable:** Updated studio scoring, tests, proposed or approved pipeline breakdown, and a list of any previously scored prospects requiring re-evaluation.

**STOP after Phase 1.**

## PHASE 2 — Individualized English emails, follow-ups and replies

**Objective:** Eliminate generic, repeated email templates. Every message must be individually composed for its actual recipient.

This phase covers:

- Initial outreach.
- Follow-up emails.
- Responses to incoming emails.
- Re-engagement messages.

It covers both indie game developers and animation studios.

### Required behaviour

Every email must be written around the actual prospect and available research evidence.

The agent must consider:

- The prospect's actual work.
- Their portfolio and projects.
- Their services and capabilities.
- Relevant production or development activities.
- The most appropriate Studio Kairegi service.
- Previous outreach and actual conversation history.

For example, an indie game developer may need anime-style 2D promotional animation, while an animation studio may be a potential client for layout, key animation, in-betweens or coloring support.

These are possible approaches, not reusable templates.

### No superficial personalization

The following do not count as personalization:

- Changing the recipient's name.
- Changing the company name.
- Changing the location.
- Replacing a few words with synonyms.
- Rotating between a small collection of fixed templates.

The actual message, reasoning, opening, collaboration angle and wording must be developed for the individual recipient.

### Replies and follow-ups

- Read and understand the actual incoming message.
- Respond to the question or issue raised.
- Use the real conversation history.
- Never claim that a prospect expressed interest when they did not.
- Never repeat the initial pitch mechanically.
- Never use the same generic reply for unrelated prospects.
- Keep consequential replies under the existing human-approval workflow.

### Similarity and quality controls

Implement:

- Exact duplicate detection.
- Near-duplicate detection.
- Name-swapping and company-swapping detection.
- Structural repetition detection.
- Regeneration or manual review when a draft is insufficiently individualized.

Similarity thresholds must be established through tests, not arbitrary guesses.

Do not use random synonym replacement to manufacture uniqueness.

### Evidence requirements

- Every personalized claim must have supporting prospect evidence.
- Record which evidence informed the draft.
- Do not invent projects, needs, staffing shortages or outsourcing intentions.
- Insufficient research must trigger additional research or manual review, never a generic fallback email.

### Acceptance criteria

Test several different prospects from both categories.

- Their messages must be substantively different.
- Name-swapped copies must fail uniqueness checks.
- Generic templates must not enter the send-ready workflow.
- Replies must address the actual incoming message.
- Follow-ups must reflect actual previous conversations.
- Insufficient evidence must trigger review.
- Existing sending controls must remain unchanged.

**Deliverable:** Individualized English drafting and reply generation, similarity checks, evidence-grounding tests and a report identifying the original cause of repeated templates.

**STOP after Phase 2.**

## PHASE 3 — Japanese-language emails, follow-ups and replies

**Objective:** Japanese prospects must receive natural, professionally written Japanese communication rather than English emails or awkward literal translations.

This is a separate phase from English individualization. Do not consider Phase 2 complete for Japanese communication.

### Required behaviour

When a prospect is Japanese and Japanese is the appropriate communication language:

- Initial outreach must be written in Japanese.
- Follow-ups must be written in Japanese.
- Replies must be written in Japanese.
- Re-engagement messages must be written in Japanese.

The agent must determine the appropriate language using available evidence, such as the studio's website language, business communication language and previous correspondence.

An existing conversation in Japanese must continue in Japanese unless the recipient indicates otherwise.

### Japanese writing quality

Messages must:

- Use natural Japanese business communication.
- Use appropriate professional politeness.
- Use correct anime-production terminology.
- Distinguish correctly between レイアウト, 原画, 動画, 仕上げ and related production terminology.
- Avoid unnatural direct translations from English.
- Avoid excessive formality or awkward machine-translated expressions.
- Use Japanese company and recipient names accurately.
- Preserve the actual meaning of Studio Kairegi's service offering.

### Individualization

All Phase 2 uniqueness requirements also apply in Japanese.

- Japanese emails must be individually composed.
- Japanese replies must reflect actual incoming messages.
- Name-swapped and near-duplicate Japanese drafts must be detected.
- Similarity checks must work with Japanese text and must not rely exclusively on space-separated word comparison.
- Insufficient evidence must trigger research or review.

### Testing

Use representative Japanese animation studios and actual or representative Japanese business correspondence.

Evaluate:

- Naturalness.
- Grammar.
- Politeness.
- Industry terminology.
- Factual accuracy.
- Personalization.
- Correct names.
- Japanese similarity detection.

Do not claim Japanese quality is acceptable merely because the output contains Japanese characters.

**Deliverable:** Japanese-language generation and reply handling, language-selection logic, Japanese uniqueness tests and a quality evaluation report.

**STOP after Phase 3.**

## PHASE 4 — Improve discovery of leads and suitable studios

**Objective:** Find more relevant potential clients without relying on recruitment announcements, outsourcing advertisements or manually supplied websites.

### Discovery targets

Primary:

- Indie game developers.

Secondary:

- Animation studios suitable for production support.

### Required behaviour

Expand discovery using relevant independent public sources and evidence of actual work.

Preserve existing working sources, including:

- Wikidata.
- AniList.
- GDELT.
- RSS and feeds.
- Common Crawl.
- Public studio websites and portfolios.

Investigate additional discovery opportunities through:

- Studio portfolios.
- Production credits.
- Published animation works.
- Animation production company listings.
- Game development projects.
- Publicly documented studio activity.

### Studio discovery must not depend on hiring

A studio must not be excluded merely because it:

- Has no recruitment page.
- Is not hiring.
- Has no outsourcing announcement.
- Has a small team.
- Has only a limited number of published projects.

### Japanese discovery

Use valid English and Japanese discovery terms.

Examples:

- Animation studio.
- Anime production studio.
- 2D animation studio.
- Animation production company.
- アニメーション制作会社
- アニメ制作会社
- 作画スタジオ
- 2Dアニメーション
- 制作実績

Validate Wikidata classes against actual labels before adding them.

### Discovery quality

- Deduplicate candidates across sources.
- Preserve source provenance.
- Validate official websites.
- Reject social-only pages as official websites.
- Support pagination.
- Avoid repeatedly discovering the same entities.
- Preserve source registry, quotas and compliance controls.
- Do not require the user to manually provide studio URLs.

### Acceptance criteria

- Relevant small studios can be discovered.
- Portfolio-based discovery works.
- Studios without hiring signals remain discoverable.
- English and Japanese discovery terms work.
- Cross-source duplicates are controlled.
- Existing source controls cannot be bypassed.

**Deliverable:** Expanded discovery pipeline, validated source/term configuration, tests and discovery coverage report.

## PHASE 5 — Contactability and email verification

**Status: Completed (local implementation and regression tests passed).**

**Objective:** Establish whether a discovered prospect has an acceptable business email contact.

Contactability must remain separate from suitability scoring.

### Required outcomes

| Contact situation                                    | Result       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Verified studio business email                       | Pass         |
| Verified employee business email belonging to studio | Pass         |
| Email found but ownership or validity uncertain      | Needs Review |
| No email                                             | Rejected     |
| Invalid or bounced email                             | Rejected     |
| Contact form only                                    | Rejected     |

### Requirements

Distinguish:

- Syntax validity.
- Source provenance.
- Official domain association.
- Employee affiliation.
- Deliverability evidence.
- Bounce and complaint status.

A published email address must not automatically be called verified.

Define the verification standard before implementation. Never fabricate mailbox verification.

Generic addresses such as `info@` must follow the same evidence rules as named addresses.

**Deliverable:** Separate contactability decision, evidence record, tests and an explanation of what constitutes verification.

**STOP after Phase 5.**
